# Tagging community groups: guide for Ambassadors

Draft for owner review (Add-on A4, task CG-8). Do not use it for real tagging until the owner signs off the calibration set in `docs/groups_calibration_set.md` (A4-Q7).

## 1. What you are doing and why

The Atlas shows which organisations work on plastics, what they do, and where they work. Volunteer Ambassadors add these facts. We call them tags.

Please keep these rules in mind:

- You tag **organisations only**. Individuals and single-person practices are not listed.
- Tags come from **public evidence**. Use what the group says on its own pages. Do not use what you already know about the group.
- Never guess. If the page does not say it, write `not_stated`.
- Each tag needs a link, a date and a short quote. See section 5.
- **Being on the BFFP list does not show plastics focus.** BFFP membership is a neutral fact that the Atlas shows as provenance. Never use it to decide a tag.
- We do not score or rank groups. Your tags describe each group. They do not judge it.
- No computer tool writes tags. You write them. The Atlas checks the form and the rules, and a reviewer checks your work.

## 2. First: is this an organisation?

Check this before anything else. Ask yourself:

- Is this a **collective**? Does it have a shared name and purpose, and more than one person doing the work, such as members, staff, volunteers or trustees?

If yes, go on to section 3.

If no, the entry is **not an organisation**. Examples:

- one person's consultancy, freelance practice or sole-trader business
- one person's personal blog or newsletter
- a personal social-media profile (a page for one person, not for a group)

What to do: choose the reason `not_an_organisation` and stop. Do not fill any other field. Do not write the person's name or any personal detail in a note.

A social-media page counts as evidence only when the group has no site of its own. A personal profile is never an organisation, even if it posts about plastics.

## 3. The five-minute path

Look at the group's own channels in this order:

1. **Home page.** Read the mission line and the main menu.
2. **About or Mission page.** Read what the group says it is for and who runs it.
3. **Programmes or Campaigns page.** Read what it actually does.
4. **Site search for "plastic".** Also try "plastics" and, if you read the language, the local word for plastic.

Stop after about **five minutes**. The limit is a guide, not a target. A short, clear site may take two minutes. Do not search the wider web. Use the group's own channels first, then an official registry, then a reliable independent source.

If you cannot decide in five minutes, choose `cannot_determine` and one reason:

| Reason | Use it when |
|---|---|
| `no_web_presence` | the group has no website or official channel you can find |
| `dead_link` | the site or its main link does not load |
| `language_barrier` | you cannot read the site well enough (see section 7) |
| `insufficient_information` | the site loads but does not say enough to tag |
| `not_an_organisation` | section 2 applies |
| `conflict_of_interest` | section 7 applies |

## 4. The Tier 1 fields

Every tagged entry needs these fields: entity type, sector, plastics centrality, primary activities, geographic scope and places, and evidence. If you know the languages of the group's pages, add them too (section 6).

Use the exact value names below. The meanings are short. If a value does not fit, choose `other` and add a note.

### 4.1 Entity type (`entity_type`)

Pick one.

- `organisation`: a formal body with its own name and governance.
- `network_coalition`: a group of member organisations that work together.
- `programme_project`: a named programme or project run by a host body.
- `campaign`: a named campaign on one issue, often time-bound.
- `informal_group`: a collective group without formal registration.
- `chapter_branch`: a local branch of a larger body. If the parent is on the roster, the form asks you for the parent.
- `other`: none of the above fits.

### 4.2 Sector (`sector`)

The sector describes what kind of body the group is. It does not describe the topic.

- `ngo_civil_society`: a non-profit or civil society body.
- `community_informal`: an unregistered local community group.
- `foundation`: a grant-making or endowed foundation.
- `academia_research`: a university or research institute.
- `private_sector`: a business or industry body.
- `government`: a government body.
- `joint_power_authority`: a joint-power authority.
- `igo`: an intergovernmental organisation.
- `other`: none of the above fits.

### 4.3 Plastics centrality (`plastics_centrality`)

This is the most important judgement. It describes how central plastics is to the group's own work.

| Value | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| `primary` | plastics (pollution, waste, policy, production or health) is the group's stated core mission or flagship programme |
| `substantial` | plastics is one of up to three named programme areas, or the group has its own plastics campaign, page or report |
| `incidental` | plastics is mentioned in passing, in a long list of topics, or only in a membership statement |
| `none` | the group does not mention plastics after the five-minute checks |
| `cannot_determine` | the site is down or cannot be read |

Rules that apply to every group:

- BFFP membership alone never sets a level.
- Rate what the group says about its own work.
- Some groups do relevant work but never use plastics words. For example, a group may work on fenceline justice near petrochemical plants. Choose `substantial` only if the group links that work to plastics or plastic production in its own words. Otherwise choose `none` and write a short note.
- If you are unsure, say so in the note. A reviewer makes the final call.

### 4.4 Primary activities (`primary_activities`)

Choose **one to five** values. List the most central first. The first value is the group's principal activity, and the Atlas uses it in its charts.

Record what the group says it does, not what it supports. A group that gives grants to others is a funder, even if it cares about litter.

- `advocacy_campaigning`: public campaigns and pressure for change
- `policy_governance`: laws, rules, policy design or governance work
- `research_monitoring`: surveys, monitoring or data
- `research_toxicology`: research concerning toxicity testing
- `research_other`: additional research activities
- `education_awareness`: schools, public information or outreach
- `cleanup_removal`: litter pick-ups and removal of waste from land or water
- `waste_management_recycling`: collection, sorting or recycling services
- `reuse_refill_alternatives`: refill, reuse systems or alternative materials
- `business_innovation`: products, business models or technology
- `community_organising_justice`: organising local communities, or justice work
- `coalition_building_capacity`: networks, training or building capacity for others
- `funding_grantmaking`: giving grants or funds
- `legal_action`: litigation or legal challenges
- `other`: none of the above fits (add a note)

Example: a group runs beach clean-ups and then teaches schools about litter. Record `cleanup_removal` first and `education_awareness` second.

### 4.5 Geographic scope and places (`geographic_scope`, `places`)

Scope is the widest area the group claims for **its own work**. Do not use the area of its network.

- `local`: one town, city or neighbourhood
- `subnational`: a state, province or region inside one country
- `national`: one country
- `regional_multi_country`: several countries in one region
- `global`: work across the world

Places are codes for the areas the group's own work names. Use up to five.

- Countries: ISO 3166-1 two-letter codes, for example `US` or `NO`.
- Subnational areas: ISO 3166-2 codes, for example `US-CA`.

### 4.6 Evidence (`evidence`)

Each Tier 1 facet needs its own evidence entry. Section 5 explains how to write it.

## 5. The evidence rule

For each facet you fill, record:

- **url**: the web address of the page you used.
- **accessed_at**: the date you read the page, as `YYYY-MM-DD`. Autofill this for the user based on current date.
- **quote**: the exact words from the page that support the value. Use **25 words or fewer**. The quote for **plastics centrality** is different: use **10 to 50 words** (and at most 600 characters), and it cannot be `not_stated` for a tagged group. Copy the words in the page's own language. Do not translate, correct or reword them. If the quote is too long, choose a shorter part of the page.
- Or **not_stated**, if the page does not support a value.

You may add:

- an **English gloss** in the note field, up to 200 characters, so that reviewers who do not read the language can follow it
- an archived copy of the page, if you have one

The quote must be on the page at that URL. A reviewer checks this. If the page changes later, read it again and record the new date.

## 6. Tier 2 fields (optional)

Fill these only when the group's own pages say so. If you are not sure, leave the field empty.

- **Legal form** (`legal_form`): `registered_nonprofit`, `unincorporated`, `company`, `public_body`, `university` or `unknown`. Add a short note with the evidence, for example a registration number printed on the site.
- **Participation in science** (`participation_in_science`): use only if the group does or hosts monitoring or research. Values, from least to most shared design: `contractual`, `contributory`, `collaborative`, `co_created`, `collegial`, or `not_applicable`. Scientists design contributory projects, and the public mainly supplies data. Collaborative and co-created projects share design work with the public (Bonney et al. 2009). The 2012 participation framework adds contractual and collegial contributions (Shirk et al. 2012).
- **Themes** (`themes`): theme tags from the form's list, only when the group's own pages state them.
- **Analytic lens** (`analytic_lens`): leave empty unless a reviewer asks.
- **Constituency** (`constituency`): only when the group describes itself that way (for example, `youth` or `indigenous`).
- **Stated affiliations** (`stated_affiliations`): record a relation only when the group itself states it on its own pages. The relations are `member_of`, `partner_of`, `fiscal_sponsor`, `parent_of` and `funded_by`. The other party must be an organisation. Never infer a relation from a logo or a list of partners on another site.
- **Website languages** (`website_languages`): the language codes (ISO 639-1, for example `en` or `no`) of the languages the group's own pages use.
- **External codes**: do not type these. The Atlas looks them up.

## 7. Language, corrections and credit


**Language.** Tag a group only if you can read its pages well enough to quote them accurately. If you cannot, do not guess. Either choose `cannot_determine` with `language_barrier`, or mark the entry **needs language support**. The coordinator then sends it to an Ambassador who reads that language.

**Corrections.** If you find a mistake in a published tag, use the correction channel that the coordinator gives you. Do not edit published records yourself.

**Credit.** Your contributions are under the Ambassador contribution terms, version 1, which use the CC BY-NC 4.0 licence. By default, your name is shown with your approved contributions, for example "Confirmed by" followed by your name. You can switch this off at any time in Notification settings. Your contributions then say "Confirmed by a local ambassador". Your ambassador ID is never published, and your email address is never published.

## 8. Two worked examples (fictional)

The two organisations below are **invented**. They are fictional and do not describe any real group. The pages and quotes are made up for practice.

### Example A: Lantern Bay Water Alliance (fictional)

The home page says: "Lantern Bay Water Alliance keeps our bay clean for everyone who swims, fishes and surfs there." The About page says: "We are a registered charity run by eight trustees and volunteers." The Programmes page lists "Plastic Free Harbour", with its own page: "Plastic Free Harbour runs monthly beach clean-ups and school workshops on plastic litter."

| Field | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation check | pass | it has trustees and volunteers, and a registered charity status |
| `entity_type` | `organisation` | a registered charity with its own governance |
| `sector` | `ngo_civil_society` | a non-profit body |
| `plastics_centrality` | `substantial` | the home page is about the bay, not plastics, so plastics is not the core mission. But "Plastic Free Harbour" is one named programme area with its own page |
| `primary_activities` | `cleanup_removal`, `education_awareness` | clean-ups come first in the programme, then school workshops |
| `geographic_scope` | `local` | one bay and its town |
| `places` | `US-CA` | the bay is in one state (fictional) |
| `legal_form` (Tier 2) | `registered_nonprofit` | the About page says "registered charity" |

Evidence (abridged):

- `plastics_centrality`: url of the Programmes page; accessed on the date you read it; quote "Plastic Free Harbour runs monthly beach clean-ups and school workshops on plastic litter" (13 words).
- `primary_activities`: the same page and quote.

### Example B: Fenwick Neighbours Association (fictional)

The About page says: "We are a group of neighbours. Our work covers housing, parking, parks, noise, traffic and plastic bags on our streets." There are no programme pages. The site search for "plastic" finds only this sentence.

| Field | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation check | pass | a group of neighbours with a shared name and several volunteers |
| `entity_type` | `informal_group` | no registration is stated |
| `sector` | `community_informal` | an unregistered local community group |
| `plastics_centrality` | `incidental` | plastics appears only once, in a long list of topics |
| `primary_activities` | `community_organising_justice`, `advocacy_campaigning` | the group organises neighbours and raises local issues |
| `geographic_scope` | `local` | one neighbourhood |
| `legal_form` (Tier 2) | `unincorporated` | no registration is stated |

Evidence (abridged):

- `plastics_centrality`: the About page URL; accessed on the date you read it; quote "housing, parking, parks, noise, traffic and plastic bags on our streets" (11 words).

Note the lesson of this example: a single mention in a list of topics is `incidental`, not `substantial`. Do not raise the level because plastic bags are a real local concern.

## 9. Quick checklist before you submit

- Is it a collective organisation? If not, use `not_an_organisation`.
- Did you stop after about five minutes?
- Does every value use the exact name from this guide?
- Does each facet have a URL, a date and a quote of 25 words or fewer (plastics centrality: 10 to 50 words), or `not_stated` (not for plastics centrality on a tagged group)?
- Did you record only what the group's own pages say?
- Do you have a conflict of interest? If so, stop.
- Is the language one you can read? If not, mark needs language support.
