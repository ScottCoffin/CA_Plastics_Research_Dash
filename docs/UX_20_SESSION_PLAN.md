# Moderated usability sessions: plan and script

Prepared 2026-11-12 for `docs/EM_spec.md` UX-20. The agent prepared these materials only. The owner
recruits participants and runs the sessions; nothing here has been run or simulated.

## Goal
Find where real users of the Atlas get stuck on six core tasks. This complements the automated
checks (UX-17, UX-18) and the manual accessibility pass (UX-19), which cannot show whether people
understand what they see.

## Participants (5)
Recruit from the Atlas's actual audiences, one or two per group:

| Slot | Audience | Example source |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | State or regional agency staff (water boards, DTSC, OPC, OEHHA) | Owner's agency contacts |
| P2 | Academic researcher working on plastics | A California lab listed in Experts |
| P3 | Graduate student or early-career researcher | University plastics or environmental programme |
| P4 | Journalist or science writer | Environmental desk at a California outlet |
| P5 | NGO or community-science staff | Groups that run beach or river surveys |

Screen for: has not used the Atlas before; can join a 45-minute video call with screen sharing;
includes at least one person who mainly uses a phone and, if possible, one who uses a screen reader
or keyboard only.

## Data handling (no consent form needed)
- Collect no personal data beyond a first name or alias and the audience group.
- Do not record audio or video. The facilitator takes written notes only.
- If the owner later decides to record, add a written consent step first; this plan does not cover it.

## Session outline (45 minutes)
| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Welcome and think-aloud practice |
| 5-35 min | Six tasks (about 5 minutes each; stop a task at 7 minutes) |
| 35-42 min | Debrief questions |
| 42-45 min | Thanks and close |

Start every session at https://plastiverse.live/ on the participant's own browser and device. Since
13 November 2026 the interactive app is the home page; the older tabbed site is retired and its
addresses (including `/app/` links) redirect to the matching page.

## Facilitator script

### Welcome (read aloud)
"Thanks for helping. We are testing the website, not you, so nothing you do is wrong. Please think
aloud as you go: say what you are looking for, what you expect to happen and what surprises you.
I will mostly stay quiet. If you get stuck, say so; it is useful to us. You can stop at any time.
We are not recording; I am only taking notes."

### Think-aloud practice
"To practise, please look at this page and tell me what you think this site is for."

### Tasks (read each exactly; do not name buttons or menus)
1. "Find a researcher whose work is led from California, and open their full profile."
2. "Show only experts working on one topic you care about, from 2020 onwards. Download that list,
   and check that the file has the same number of people the page says."
3. "On the institutions view, find two institutions that are in the same tier. Explain, using only
   what the page tells you, why they share a tier."
4. "Find the monitoring observations for one kind of sample, such as water or sediment, and
   download them."
5. "Look at funding for California-led awards. What does the site say about how NIH award amounts
   are counted?"
6. "Pick one institution you know. Open its own page and find who has led the most of its
   plastics papers, and which topic it publishes on most."
7. Optional, if time allows: "Find a researcher you know on the collaboration map, and name two
   people they have written papers with."

Task 6 replaced the old "get back to the new version from the older site" task when the older site
was retired (13 November 2026). Task 7 covers the collaboration map, which UX-18's automated tasks
do not exercise; skip it if the session runs long.

Neutral prompts only: "What are you thinking?", "What would you do next?", "What did you expect
there?" Do not answer questions about the site during a task; note them instead.

### Debrief questions
1. "Which task was hardest, and why?"
2. "Was there a word or number on the site you did not understand?"
3. "Would you trust these numbers in your own work? What would make you trust them more?"
4. "What did you expect to find here that was missing?"

## After the sessions
- Count completions per task; list each problem seen by two or more participants first.
- For each problem: route, what the participant did, what they expected, and a proposed fix.
- Compare with the automated task results in `docs/UX_18_RESULTS.md`; a task that passes there but
  fails here usually points to wording, not a broken control.
